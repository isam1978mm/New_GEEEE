# Allen Harbor Landfill cap-depth pair result - 2026-07-27

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO**

Allen Harbor is a useful two-cover-system candidate, but it does not provide the two final measured as-built depth zones required by the approved numerical-depth plan.

## What passed

- The landfill covers approximately 15 acres.
- Construction of the remedial cap was completed in August 1999.
- The completed remedy contains two mapped cover systems:
  - a multimedia cap above the 14-foot mean-sea-level contour;
  - a soil cap below that elevation and in the northern extension.
- Both areas were graded and vegetated.
- Long-term inspections and settlement surveys support a stable whole-site post-closure condition, including a usable Sentinel-1 period in principle.

## What the records actually prove

The official descriptions state that the multimedia cap contains multiple soil and geosynthetic components. The design description includes an 18-inch barrier-protection layer and a 6-inch vegetative-support layer above the impermeable components.

For the soil-cap area, the original cover was regraded and supplemented with:

- 18 inches of common borrow fill;
- 6 inches of soil supporting vegetation.

These descriptions explain why the completed site is often summarized as having an approximately 3-foot multimedia cap and an approximately 2-foot soil cap. They are not final measured as-built depth records.

- March 1998 Final Design Analysis Report for Closure of the Allen Harbor Landfill;

Neither report appears in the recovered official collection listings. The available records contain the Record of Decision, Explanation of Significant Differences and later reviews, but not the construction measurement package.

## Fatal blockers

### 1. No final measured as-built depths

No certified table or drawing was found that measures the completed thickness of either cap system. The published numbers are layer requirements or construction summaries.

### 2. No numerical uncertainty

No project-specific horizontal or vertical survey uncertainty was found for the cap thicknesses.

Annual settlement surveys measure later movement at monitoring wells, gas vents and shoreline structures. They do not measure the original constructed thickness of the two caps.

### 3. Matching near-surface construction is not proven

Both surfaces are vegetated, but the records do not prove that the complete radar-facing upper soil assembly has the same material source, compaction and moisture behavior across the multimedia and soil-cap areas.

The underlying systems are intentionally different: the multimedia cap contains drainage and impermeable components, while the soil cap does not.

### 4. Two clean measured interiors cannot be certified

The soil cap follows the lower-elevation shoreline and northern extension. Without final measured-depth polygons and exact construction survey geometry, a clean 30-40 m interior cannot be established conservatively for both conditions.
