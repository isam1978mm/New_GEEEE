# Aurora Soil Capping Study depth screen result

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO**

Aurora has excellent scale and replication, but the public record does not provide final measured as-built depth labels or a defensible depth-only matched pair.

## What the public record proves

- The study contains **36 one-hectare cells**.
- Twelve alternate cover designs were replicated three times.
- Surface coversoil depths include **10, 20 and 30 cm**.
- Surface materials include peat-mineral mix and forest-floor mineral mix.
- The cells were randomly placed across the study watershed.
- Public research papers provide an approximate site location and a treatment-layout figure.

## Fatal blockers

### 1. Construction evidence is not publicly recoverable

The accessible papers repeatedly cite a 2017 O'Kane Consultants report for the cover-system design and monitoring details. A publicly retrievable copy of that report was not found.

The accessible record therefore does not establish:

- final measured as-built thickness values;
- numerical thickness uncertainty;
- construction survey accuracy;
- a survey-coordinate or GIS deliverable linking measured depths to exact cell polygons.

The 10, 20 and 30 cm figures remain treatment-design labels rather than independent final measured depth references.

### 2. Depth is not the only treatment difference

The study also varies:

- peat-mineral versus forest-floor mineral surface material;
- underlying subsoil material;
- subsoil-layer arrangement;
- tree species and planting density;
- fertilizer treatment in later studies.

The public record does not identify a pair for which thickness is the only material radar-facing difference and for which final measured depth has been independently certified.
