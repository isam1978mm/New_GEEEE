# Blocker 2 — Stage-1 Bridgeton Sanitary Landfill Rejection — 2026-07-21

Status: Stage 1 screened. Bridgeton Sanitary Landfill, Missouri, is classified as `rejected_for_depth_calibration`. Blocker 2 remains unresolved. No calibration-pack intake, training, or app-depth enablement is authorized from this site.

## Verified official public facts

- Missouri DNR documents a subsurface smoldering event beginning no later than 2010, with elevated temperatures, hydrogen, carbon monoxide, odor impacts, and corrective actions.
- The official site history states subsidence began exceeding expected levels in 2012.
- The South Quarry EVOH cap was completed in August 2013, with additional EVOH placement later in 2013.
- North Quarry grading and EVOH-cover work continued during 2016–2017, including leachate-sump replacement and new monitoring infrastructure.
- The official record also documents repeated soil placement, drainage grading, gas/leachate-system changes, cap repairs, and continuing landfill monitoring.
- Missouri DNR publicly lists quarterly North Quarry settlement-monitoring drawings for 2020 and first-quarter 2021, quarterly slope/stability reports, infrastructure as-built drawings, monthly settlement-front maps, and extensive corrective-action reporting.

## Evidence/access limitations

- No single clean pre-cap surface, one-time final as-built surface, and later repeat surface could be isolated from the continuing remediation record.

## Decision

Reject Bridgeton Sanitary Landfill from the current depth-calibration screen.

The site has unusually rich settlement and as-built reporting, but the measured elevation changes cannot be interpreted as a clean response to one cap-placement event. They overlap a subsurface smoldering event, abnormal subsidence, staged membrane-cover work, repeated grading and soil placement, gas/leachate construction, drainage work, and cap/infrastructure repairs. That combination makes the surface signal scientifically unsuitable for direct cap-thickness or relative-depth calibration.

The site may still be useful later as a negative-control or confounded-settlement example, but not as a positive direct-depth reference.
