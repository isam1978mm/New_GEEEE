# Blocker 2 — Stage-1 Cocopah Landfill Candidate — 2026-07-21

Status: Stage 1 active. Cocopah Landfill, Arizona, is retained as `candidate_under_review`. Blocker 2 remains unresolved. No calibration-pack intake, training, or app-depth enablement is authorized.

## Verified official public facts

- EPA's August 28, 2020 final rule states that waste was last received on June 30, 2000 and interim closure construction was completed in 2003 with an interim three-foot monolithic soil cover.
- The landfill property covers approximately 192 acres; approximately 138 acres received waste in a 105-acre North Fill Area and a 33-acre South Fill Area, with approximately 2.5 million tons of waste in place.
- EPA approved an alternative final cover for a 135-acre site. The approved system is a minimum three-foot multilayer cover: a minimum 30-inch infiltration layer plus a six-inch erosion layer on the top deck, with rock-armored side slopes.
- The approved work includes top-deck grading, South Fill Area slope regrading, drainage benches on portions of the North Fill Area, surface-water drainage structures, gas-probe extensions, survey monuments, berms, and related engineered fill.
- The November 2019 closure CQA plan requires the finished final-cover surface to be surveyed on a 150-foot grid after placement of the final cover and erosion layer so that an as-built drawing can be prepared.
- The CQA plan also provides for final reporting, record drawings, field-density testing, and possible potholing to verify cover thickness.
- The EPA docket material located during this screen is design/CQA-plan material. Targeted public searches did not locate a completed final CQA report, certified record drawings, the native 150-foot-grid survey, a closure-completion certification, explicit survey accuracy, or a later repeat topographic/settlement survey.
- The official PDF text was accessible, but screenshot rendering of the CQA pages failed with a cache-miss error. Therefore, this record relies on the parsed official PDF text and does not claim visual verification of the drawings.

## Decision

Retain Cocopah. It is one of the strongest public design-level leads because the cap footprint is very large, waste remained in place, the cover thickness is specified, and the CQA plan requires an actual surveyed as-built surface on a defined grid.

Do not promote it. Approval and a CQA plan do not prove that construction was completed or that the required survey package exists. The closure also includes slope regrading, drainage, gas, road, and berm work, so a clean Sentinel-1 test would need an isolated cap-only footprint.

Then determine whether the top deck or either fill area can be isolated from the drainage, gas, road, and slope-regrading work.
