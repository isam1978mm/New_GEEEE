# Colesville Municipal Landfill - Dynamic Cap Follow-up

Date: 2026-07-25

## Decision

**Status:** closed as a numerical-depth calibration candidate.

Colesville has unusually detailed cap design documents, but it is not a stable known-depth reference. The public record shows that the cap was designed to accommodate substantial settlement, required annual surveying and regrading, and later underwent liner, slope, cap, and gas-vent repairs. The buried waste is also a heterogeneous landfill mass rather than a discrete reference target.

## What is confirmed

- Site: Colesville Municipal Landfill, Broome County, New York; NYSDEC Site 704010.
- The landfill cap remedial action was constructed during the 1990s; EPA records construction completion in 2004 for the full site remedy.
- The final-cover design included, from top to bottom:
  - 6 inches of topsoil;
  - 24 inches of barrier-protection material;
  - a 40-mil flexible membrane liner;
  - a gas-venting layer beneath the liner.
- Depending on the chosen alternative, the barrier-protection layer included either a 12-inch granular drainage layer plus 12 inches of soil, or a geosynthetic drainage layer plus 24 inches of soil.
- The waste boundary was investigated using approximately 100 test pits, but areas without test pits were defined using visible waste and historical information.
- The design predicted short-term settlement of approximately 0.7 to 1.1 feet and long-term settlement of approximately 1.0 to 1.9 feet in critical sections.
- The design required annual surveys of cap slopes and regrading wherever settlement caused ponding.
- The public archive contains a 2005 settlement investigation, 2007 slope repair, 2010 cap-regrading report, and 2019 landfill-gas-vent repair record.
- The 2005 investigation exposed the flexible liner at multiple locations and documented liner repair and backfilling.
- EPA's current site record continues to list cap operation and maintenance, slope stabilization, drainage/erosion control, landfill-gas management, and monitoring.

## Why it is not usable

1. The 30-inch design thickness above the liner is not a measured target depth to the landfill waste. The gas-venting layer, prepared subgrade, regrading fill, and irregular waste surface vary below the liner.
2. The waste boundary is partly interpretive rather than a complete survey-grade excavation boundary.
3. The design explicitly anticipated large and spatially varying settlement.
4. Actual cap disturbances and repairs continued after Sentinel-1 became available, including gas-vent repairs in 2019.
5. No exact subarea has a documented measured cover depth with numerical survey uncertainty and a proven unchanged radar interval.
6. The reference material is a heterogeneous municipal and industrial landfill mass, not a discrete object or uniform calibration body.
