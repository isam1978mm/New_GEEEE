# Detour Lake Mine cover-trial depth screen result

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO**

The Detour Lake trial is large and contains intentionally different cover-depth designs, but the public evidence does not provide a defensible measured numerical-depth pair under the locked rules.

## What the public record proves

- The trial covers approximately **10 hectares**.
- It contains **12 bench-slope plots and one plateau plot**.
- The treatment design includes cover thicknesses of **0, 0.3, 0.7 and 1.0 m**.
- Construction used mine-scale equipment during 2018-2019.
- Monitoring continued through 2024.
- The trial also varies slope angle, aspect, cover composition, surface grading, ripping and revegetation.

## Fatal blockers

### 1. No final measured depth labels

The accessible five-year review describes the cover thickness treatments, but it does not publish:

- final measured as-built thickness values for each plot;
- a pointwise thickness survey;
- numerical construction or survey uncertainty;
- surveyed corner coordinates or a georeferenced GIS/CAD plot deliverable.

The 0.3, 0.7 and 1.0 m values therefore remain treatment-design labels rather than independently measured numerical depth references.

The 2022 construction paper could not be recovered from the two public UBC download endpoints. Both returned an HTML blocking page instead of PDF bytes. This decision does not claim that the inaccessible paper was fully inspected.

### 2. No matched thickness-only pair

Depth is not the only controlled difference between the plots. The trial also varies:

- slope and aspect;
- peat-mineral composition;
- surface grading;
- ripping;
- seeding and planting prescriptions;
- upstream run-on conditions.

The five-year review states that as-constructed peat content differed from design. Sloped areas received little or no peat, while plateau peat content was highly variable. These differences materially affect vegetation and surface radar response.

### 3. Exact surfaces were disturbed or eroded

The monitoring record identifies:

- rills and gullies;
- erosion pavement;
- small sinkholes;
- waste rock exposed in gullies on the 0.3 m plot;
- large barren areas and excavator tracks from recent test-pit excavation and re-covering.

Most erosion rates later declined, but the exact candidate interiors cannot be certified as unchanged, repair-free depth zones during one clean Sentinel-1 period.
