# East Mount Zion Landfill — Ecological Revitalization Follow-up

Date: 2026-07-25

## Decision

**Status:** closed as a numerical-depth calibration route.

East Mount Zion has a completed engineered landfill cap, but its surface is not an unchanged reference. EPA records document additional methane-vent construction, intensive vegetation removal, repeated mowing, herbicide treatment, revegetation with native grasses and wildflowers, shrub planting, and a substantial groundhog population whose burrows threatened the cap. No point-specific measured final cover depth, exact usable subarea geometry, or numerical uncertainty was recovered.

## What is confirmed

- Site: East Mount Zion Landfill, Springettsbury Township, York County, Pennsylvania.
- The inactive landfill covers approximately 10 acres.
- The selected remedy included an impermeable cap, gas vents, surface-water controls, and fencing.
- Cap construction began in 1997 and was completed in 1999.
- Eleven additional gas vents were installed in 2007.
- Operation and maintenance continues, including grass cutting, groundwater monitoring, and methane monitoring.
- EPA's 2022 revitalization report documents invasive vegetation and a dense groundhog population whose burrows threatened cap integrity.
- Most vegetation was removed with herbicide applications in 2021 and spot treatment in 2022.
- A winter cover crop was planted in 2021.
- Native wildflowers and grasses were planted across the cap in spring 2022.
- Additional perimeter and wetland shrubs were planted.
- The mowing and landscape-management regime was deliberately changed as part of the ecological-conversion project.
- The latest five-year review was completed on May 17, 2022; the next is scheduled for 2027.

## Why it is not usable

1. No public point-specific measured depth to the top of waste or another mapped reference feature was recovered.
2. No numerical construction or measurement uncertainty was found.
3. The surface was intentionally transformed during 2021–2022 through herbicide treatment, mowing, cover-crop planting, native revegetation, and shrub planting.
4. Groundhog burrows created local disturbance and a documented cap-integrity concern.
5. Additional gas vents, monitoring infrastructure, drainage controls, and maintenance-access areas create radar confounders.
6. A broad design or construction thickness could not be assigned safely to individual Sentinel-1 pixels or features.
7. Any pre-revitalization and post-revitalization observations would represent different surface conditions.
