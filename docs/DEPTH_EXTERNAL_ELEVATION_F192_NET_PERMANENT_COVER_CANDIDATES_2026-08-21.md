# F192 — Net-permanent-cover candidate screen

Date: 2026-08-21

## Purpose

Run one breadth-first public-record screen for sites where a documented final cover/cap could leave a **net, permanent elevation change** large enough to test by later pre/post elevation differencing.

This step does **not** inspect lidar, DEMs, point clouds, aerial imagery, or elevation values.

## Controlling geometry rule

A candidate is useful only if the construction can leave a net, permanent elevation change attributable to the documented target layer.

Therefore:

- `closure by removal` is **not automatically rejected in principle**, but it fails this screen when excavation/backfill/restoration makes the final elevation change non-attributable to one documented thickness;
- `closure in place` is **not automatically accepted** when substantial regrading/backfill occurs before the cap;
- the target cover must be at least 0.6 m nominal thickness for this screen;
- the qualifying construction/completion timestamp must fall in 2016–2023;
- no outbound request is allowed;
- no candidate gets more than three shallow public-source fingerprints;
- research stops at the first failing gate.

## F192 result

22 public candidates/subunits were screened breadth-first.

### Provisional survivors to F193

Only four remain worth a metadata-only lidar-bracket check:

1. CWM Arlington Landfill L-12, Oregon — 3 ft cover, March 2018 closure.
2. CWM Arlington Landfill L-13, Oregon — 3 ft cover, December 2020 closure.
3. Masonville Cove, Maryland — 2 ft maintained soil cap, documented in place by October 2019.
4. Tangerine Landfill, Arizona — 3 ft soil cover, December 2016 closure.

These are **not validated sites**. F192 only says they survived the shallow construction/date/thickness screen.

### Important re-score: Rocky Mountain Arsenal

Rocky Mountain Arsenal is not being rejected because the cover geometry is scientifically uninteresting. The public record does document 2-ft and 3-ft soil-cover areas.

It is rejected here because those covers were already documented in the 2011 remedial-action/five-year-review record, outside the frozen 2016–2023 completion window. Under the loop-breaking rules, that ends the F192 inquiry; no lidar work is performed for RMA.

### Important geometry rejects

- **North Ridge Estates:** contractors excavated 2–4 ft, then placed at least 2 ft of clean soil and restored the parcels to original use/grade. The elevation signal is therefore not a clean 2-ft permanent addition.
- **Michigan City five-pond system:** the approved method is removal of CCR plus an additional foot of underlying material. The Boiler Slag Pond was then backfilled with native sand and covered. This is exactly the kind of removal/backfill sequence that the new geometry rule is intended to reject early.
- **Gavin Stingy Run FAR:** although it is closure in place with a 2-ft final soil layer, the published plan includes regrading/backfilling onsite material before the cap. A pre/post DEM pair around the whole closure would not isolate the 2-ft cover without a separately timed pre-cap surface.
