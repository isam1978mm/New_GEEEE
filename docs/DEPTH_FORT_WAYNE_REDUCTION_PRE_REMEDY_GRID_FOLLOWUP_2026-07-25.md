# Fort Wayne Reduction Dump — Pre-Remedy Grid Follow-up

Date: 2026-07-25

## Decision

**Status:** closed as a numerical-depth calibration route.

Fort Wayne Reduction Dump has a genuine 100-foot-grid investigation of the old landfill cover, but those 36 measurements were collected before the final remedial construction. EPA later removed more than 27,000 drums, constructed soil covers across the landfill areas, installed a vertical barrier and groundwater collection trench, and completed the remedy in 1995. The old grid values therefore cannot be used as depth labels for the later Sentinel-1 surface.

## What is confirmed

- Site: Fort Wayne Reduction Dump, Fort Wayne, Indiana; EPA ID IND980679542.
- The site covers approximately 35 acres along the Maumee River floodplain.
- During the remedial investigation, the eastern landfill cover was probed on a 100-foot by 100-foot grid.
- The old cover thickness ranged from 4 to 24 inches across 36 measurements, with an average of approximately 17 inches.
- EPA's selected remedy required confirmation of cover thickness and addition of fill to achieve the required final soil-cover thickness.
- The final remedy included:
  - removal of more than 27,000 buried drums;
  - soil cover over the landfill areas;
  - erosion protection and vegetation;
  - a vertical engineered barrier;
  - a bio-polymer groundwater collection trench;
  - groundwater collection and treatment;
  - institutional controls and monitoring.
- Construction began in 1990 and was completed on September 27, 1995.
- EPA partially deleted the two capped landfill areas from the NPL in 2020.
- The July 24, 2024 Sixth Five-Year Review found the remedy protective in the short term but required additional groundwater and emerging-contaminant information for long-term protectiveness.
- The site remains in operation and maintenance.

## Why it is not usable

1. The 36 grid measurements describe the pre-remedy cover.
2. Drum excavation, fill placement, grading, soil-cover construction and vegetation changed the surface and local depth.
3. No post-construction point-specific final cover-depth table was recovered.
4. No exact as-built survey geometry or numerical construction uncertainty was recovered.
5. The western landfill includes a vertical barrier, collection trench and groundwater infrastructure that strongly confound the surface signal.
6. Floodplain erosion controls, access areas, treatment infrastructure, monitoring wells and maintenance zones require detailed exclusion geometry.
7. Partial deletion and current protectiveness establish remedy status, not survival of the old grid measurements.
