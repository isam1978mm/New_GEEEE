# Heleva Landfill — Historical Clay-Cap Follow-up

Date: 2026-07-25

## Decision

**Status:** closed as a numerical-depth calibration route.

Heleva Landfill has a stable, regulated landfill surface, but the public evidence does not provide a point-specific measured final depth with geometry and uncertainty. The commonly cited two-foot clay value refers to the operators' original 1981 closure layer. EPA later designed and installed an engineered landfill cover, so the historical two-foot statement cannot be used as the final Sentinel-1-period depth label.

## What is confirmed

- Site: Heleva Landfill Superfund Site, North Whitehall Township, Lehigh County, Pennsylvania.
- The landfill operated on about 26 acres and closed in 1981.
- The operators placed a two-foot clay layer over the waste during the original closure.
- EPA later selected and implemented an engineered landfill cover, surface-water controls, gas venting, groundwater treatment, and monitoring.
- The cap remedial action was completed in 1991 and has remained under operation and maintenance.
- EPA's current site profile states that the landfill cap and ancillary features are functioning properly and preventing direct exposure.
- The 2022 five-year review is identified as the current review, but its PDF repeatedly timed out in the available environment.
- EPA's schedule shows an additional Explanation of Significant Differences planned for September-November 2026.

## Why it is not usable

1. The two-foot clay statement is a broad historical closure description, not a surveyed local depth measurement.
2. The later EPA engineered cover changed the physical layer stack above the waste.
3. No public point table, exact cap polygon, coordinate system, final-grade survey, or layer-interface elevations were recovered.
4. No numerical measurement or construction uncertainty was found.
5. The exact relationship between the original two-foot clay layer and the final engineered cap is not documented in the accessible sources.
6. The site contains active groundwater treatment, gas vents, drainage systems, monitoring infrastructure, and other features that would require detailed exclusion geometry.
7. A planned 2026 remedy change creates an additional current-stability concern.
