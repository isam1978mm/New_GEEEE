# Himco Dump — Pre-Remedy Test-Pit Follow-up

Date: 2026-07-25

## Decision

**Status:** closed as a numerical-depth calibration route.

Himco has genuine direct cover-thickness measurements from 24 test pits, but those measurements describe the landfill before the final remedy. EPA later modified, contoured, graded and revegetated the landfill cover and installed an active landfill-gas management system. The old point measurements therefore cannot be assigned to the current Sentinel-1 surface.

## What is confirmed

- Site: Himco Dump, Elkhart County, Indiana; EPA ID IND980500292.
- The landfill operated from 1960 to 1976 and received municipal waste, construction debris, medical waste and calcium sulfate.
- The original closure consisted of sand over a calcium-sulfate layer.
- EPA excavated 24 test pits to evaluate the calcium-sulfate cover thickness.
- Fifteen of the 24 pits had less than two feet of calcium sulfate.
- Five pits had one-half foot or less.
- The 1993 selected remedy called for a composite engineered cap and active gas collection.
- EPA modified the remedy in 2004.
- The implemented remedy included contouring, grading and revegetating the existing landfill cover and installing a gas-management system.
- Final remedial action began in 2010 and construction was completed on July 19, 2012.
- The capped landfill and adjacent completed-cleanup areas were partially deleted from the NPL in 2022.
- Cap maintenance, gas-system operation, groundwater monitoring and five-year reviews continue.
- EPA records a five-year review completed on February 17, 2026.

## Why it is not usable

1. The 24 pit measurements predate the final remedial construction.
2. Later contouring, grading and revegetation changed the surface elevations and local cover thickness.
3. The final remedy includes an active gas-management system and associated infrastructure.
4. No post-construction point-specific cover-depth table or surveyed pit map was recovered.
5. No numerical uncertainty was found for either the old test pits or the final cap.
6. The old calcium-sulfate thickness is not the final depth to the top of waste under the completed remedy.
7. Partial deletion and current protectiveness establish cleanup status, not continuity of the old measured depths.
