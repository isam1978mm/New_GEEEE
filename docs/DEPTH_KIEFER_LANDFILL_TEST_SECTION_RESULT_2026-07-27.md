# Kiefer Landfill test-section result — 2026-07-27

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO**

Kiefer initially looked unusually strong because it had two side-by-side evapotranspiration-cover test sections with similar radar-facing surfaces and a documented thickness difference.

The recovered paper confirms:

- both sections were constructed in 1999;
- both used a 150 mm surface topsoil layer;
- both were seeded with the same grass mixture;
- the thin section had a 1.40 m storage layer over 0.46 m of interim cover, for 1.86 m total;
- the thick section had a 2.44 m storage layer over 0.46 m of interim cover, for 2.90 m total;
- the nominal total thickness difference was 1.04 m.

## Fatal problem

Both test sections were **decommissioned in summer 2005** after monitoring ended.

This is before the Sentinel-1 observation era. Therefore the experimental shallow/deep pair cannot be assumed to have remained intact for any usable Sentinel-1 acquisition.

The decommissioning work also included field testing, removal of large undisturbed samples, and excavation/inspection at test locations. This makes the original test configuration unsuitable as a later radar calibration target even if remnants remained.

## Geometry

Exact plot dimensions and exact WGS84 polygons were not recovered.

That missing geometry is no longer the controlling blocker because the pre-Sentinel-1 decommissioning already closes the candidate.

## Calibration decision

- Same-surface support: strong before decommissioning
- Exact depth difference: strong
- Sentinel-1-era survival: failed
- Calibration row created: no
- Earth Engine query executed: no

## Source

Smesrud, J. K., Benson, C. H., Albright, W. H., Richards, J. H., Wright, S., Israel, T., and Goodrich, K. (2012). *Using Pilot Test Data to Refine an Alternative Cover Design in Northern California*. International Journal of Phytoremediation, 14(S1), 76–93. DOI: 10.1080/15226514.2011.607871.

## Next step

Move to another completed site with:

1. measured shallow/deep or positive/negative geometry;
2. the same final radar-facing surface;
3. areas at least large enough for 20 m support;
4. confirmed survival into the Sentinel-1 period.
