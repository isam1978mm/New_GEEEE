# Lopez Canyon Landfill — Full-Scale Cover but Active Post-Closure Uses

Date: 2026-07-26

## Decision

**Status:** closed as unsuitable for depth calibration.

Lopez Canyon has a large, documented full-scale evapotranspiration cover, but it does not provide point-specific measured depth with uncertainty. The closed landfill also supports multiple active municipal and energy uses that disturb or strongly confound the surface.

## What is confirmed

- The full-scale ET cover was installed in 1999.
- The published cover profile consists of:
  - 3 feet of silty sand/clayey sand ET soil;
  - a 2-foot foundation layer beneath it.
- Cover soil was placed in 18-inch lifts and compacted to 95 percent.
- The ET cover received final regulatory approval in October 2002 after field-performance evaluation.
- California identifies post-closure uses including a heliport and a microturbine power field.
- Landfill-gas microturbines, flare-area equipment, municipal equipment, mulching operations, fleet-service facilities, and other active infrastructure have operated at the site.
- The landfill remains under an active non-operating-landfill water-quality order and continuing municipal ownership/operation.

## Why it is not usable

1. The published 3-foot and 2-foot values describe the engineered profile, not point-specific measured depth records.
2. No public as-built table, local thickness measurements, construction tolerance, or finite numerical uncertainty was recovered.
3. The site contains active post-closure infrastructure and recurring municipal operations.
4. Public evidence does not isolate an exact ET-cover polygon that remained free of gas, heliport, flare, road, drainage, equipment, and maintenance disturbance throughout a Sentinel-1 interval.
5. A broad design value cannot be assigned to individual pixels without verified as-built uniformity and an undisturbed point overlay.
