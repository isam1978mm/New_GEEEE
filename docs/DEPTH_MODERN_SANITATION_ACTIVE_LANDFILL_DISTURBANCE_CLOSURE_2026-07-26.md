# Modern Sanitation Landfill — Active Landfill and Repeated Cap Disturbance

Date: 2026-07-26

## Decision

**Status:** closed as unsuitable for numerical depth calibration.

Modern Sanitation has a completed engineered cap, but the Superfund area is embedded within an active permitted landfill property. The latest review documents truck traffic over the cap, recent reseeding, recurring filling of settlement low spots, active gas and groundwater systems, and later landfill cells over part of the original cap. The surface is not an unchanged calibration reference.

## What is confirmed

- Site: Modern Sanitation Landfill, Pennsylvania.
- EPA ID: `PAD980539068`.
- The Superfund site is the original 66-acre unlined landfill within a much larger active permitted landfill property.
- The permitted landfill contains active and inactive expansion areas and continuing waste-disposal operations.
- The original landfill cap consists of several construction areas completed between 1989 and 2000:
  - landfill slope cap/vertical expansion area;
  - 20-acre plateau cap;
  - 42-acre side-slope cap;
  - 4-acre highwall cap.
- Part of the original 66-acre landfill is now covered by later Northwest Expansion cells.
- The remedy includes active groundwater extraction, wastewater treatment, landfill-gas extraction, monitoring wells, surface-water monitoring and maintained access infrastructure.
- The 2025 Five-Year Review reports that:
  - trucks recently drove over part of the cap to reach sediment-basin work;
  - disturbed areas were reseeded;
  - minor settlement has occurred over time;
  - low spots are filled when observed to prevent ponding;
  - part of the capped area remains next to active landfill operations;
  - gas vents, monitoring probes, wells and extraction systems penetrate or occupy the landfill surface.
- The 2025 review found the remedy protective, but that finding addresses protectiveness, not calibration stability.

## Why it is not usable

1. The landfill remains an active permitted waste facility with ongoing operations and possible expansion.
2. Later landfill cells overlie part of the original capped area.
3. Truck traffic, sediment-basin work, reseeding and recurring settlement repairs alter the radar surface.
4. Active gas, groundwater and wastewater infrastructure creates spatial and temporal confounding.
5. No point-specific accepted cover-thickness table with exact survey positions and numerical uncertainty was recovered.
6. A protective cap cannot be treated as an unchanged depth reference when maintenance repeatedly modifies its surface.
