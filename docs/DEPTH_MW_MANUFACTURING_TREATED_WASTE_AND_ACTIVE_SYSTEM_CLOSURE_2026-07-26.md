# MW Manufacturing — Treated Industrial Waste and Active Groundwater System

Date: 2026-07-26

## Decision

**Status:** closed as unsuitable for numerical depth calibration.

MW Manufacturing is not a clean landfill-cover reference. The original carbon-waste pile was excavated and removed. Later engineered caps relate to treated or stabilized industrial fluff and contaminated soil, while an active groundwater treatment system and wetland setting remain. No point-specific accepted soil-cover depths, mapped measurement locations, or numerical uncertainty were recovered.

## What is confirmed

- Site: MW Manufacturing, Valley Township, Pennsylvania.
- EPA ID: `PAD980691372`.
- The approximately 15-acre former wire-reclamation facility generated carbon waste and large piles of contaminated wire-insulation fluff.
- The original carbon-waste remedy required excavation and off-site incineration/disposal rather than permanent burial under a reference soil cover.
- EPA records state that the carbon waste was excavated and shipped off site.
- Later operable units addressed contaminated groundwater, fluff waste, stabilizer material, buildings and other site features.
- Some later remedies used solidification/stabilization, containment and engineered capping.
- Construction-complete status was reached in September 2005.
- The groundwater treatment system began operating in January 2005 and long-term response activities continue.
- Wetlands and Mauses Creek form part of the site's environmental setting.
- No commercial activity has occurred on the site since 1993, but operation and maintenance remain active.
- EPA completed a Five-Year Review on August 5, 2025.

## Why it is not usable

1. The original carbon-waste target was excavated and removed, so it cannot provide a persistent buried reference.
2. Later capped material consists of treated or stabilized industrial waste, not a simple known soil-over-target geometry.
3. No public point-specific table of accepted final cover thicknesses was recovered.
4. No surveyed point geometry tied to actual local depth values was recovered.
5. No numerical construction tolerance, measurement precision, survey accuracy or finite uncertainty interval was recovered.
6. Active groundwater extraction/treatment, drainage, monitoring and wetland conditions create spatial and temporal confounding.
7. Multiple operable units and treatment histories make a single cover-depth interpretation non-unique.
