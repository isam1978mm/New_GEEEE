# NAS Alameda Site 2 cap-depth pair result - 2026-07-27

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO**

NAS Alameda Site 2 is the strongest final measured-grid candidate recovered so far. It has a large vegetated cover, hundreds of coordinate-tied final thickness measurements and one common upper-soil construction. It still fails the locked requirement for two broad, official measured depth zones with clean radar interiors and numerical uncertainty.

## What passed

- The multi-layer cover system covers approximately 79 acres.
- The final soil cover uses one common profile: 18 inches of general cover soil plus 6 inches of vegetative soil.
- The same DIAM quarry soil was used throughout the cover; compaction was the only stated difference between the general and vegetative layers.
- The surface was hydroseeded with a common erosion-control and native-species mixture.
- The completion report states that 336 verification monuments were installed on a 100-foot by 100-foot grid immediately above the animal-intrusion barrier.
- At each monument, the subgrade and completed-cover elevations were surveyed and used to calculate final cover thickness.
- Table 3-2 provides point identification, northing, easting, pre-cover elevation, post-cover elevation, settlement, final cover elevation and calculated thickness.
- Every reported location was at least 2.0 feet thick. The reported average was 2.2 feet.
- The final topographic survey was reviewed by a California-licensed professional land surveyor.

## Possible depth contrast

The measured grid contains a contiguous 5-by-3-point block with final thicknesses of approximately 2.5 to 2.8 feet. Several other contiguous blocks contain values of approximately 2.0 to 2.1 feet.

This is genuine measured variation. It is not enough by itself to create calibration zones.

## Fatal blockers

### 1. The report publishes points, not official depth polygons

The 100-foot grid verifies thickness at individual monuments. The report does not certify that the entire area between adjacent points has one depth value or depth class.

Creating shallow and deep polygons around point clusters would require interpolation or invented boundaries. The approved plan explicitly excludes scattered or point-based evidence that must be converted into unsupported polygons.

### 2. The strongest deep block overlaps infrastructure

When the deep block is placed on the official post-remedy feature drawing, it overlaps the eastern gas-vent and covered-inlet corridor. After conservative exclusion of that infrastructure, point-to-point edges and uncertainty margins, a clean 30-40 m interior cannot be established without assuming unmeasured conditions between points.

The cleanest shallow clusters are also close to perimeter or drainage infrastructure.

### 3. Project-specific numerical uncertainty is missing

The recovered completion report states the survey instruments and licensed-surveyor review but does not provide a numerical uncertainty or tolerance specifically for the 336-point final thickness measurements.

The report lists:

- Appendix J: as-built drawings and topographical survey data;
- Appendix K: Engineer of Record certification.

Those appendices were omitted from the publicly recovered report. A separate project-specific numerical accuracy statement was not found.

### 4. Stable Sentinel-1 period is not yet fully documented

The remedy includes long-term cap inspection, settlement monitoring, repairs and periodic topographic surveys. That makes stability plausible. A later official record proving a repair-free, unchanged observation period for both proposed zones was not recovered. Because the geometry and uncertainty gates already fail, no radar search was performed.
