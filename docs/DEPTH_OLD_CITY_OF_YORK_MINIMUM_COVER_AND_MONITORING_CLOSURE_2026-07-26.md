# Old City of York Landfill — Minimum Cover Specification and Monitoring Infrastructure

Date: 2026-07-26

## Decision

**Status:** closed as unsuitable for numerical depth calibration.

Old City of York Landfill includes a completed restoration of approximately 16 acres of eroded soil cover, but the public record describes the restoration only as a two-foot minimum. No point-specific accepted as-built depths, surveyed measurement locations, or numerical uncertainty were recovered. The site also contains landfill-gas vents, leachate structures, monitoring locations and a private residence on part of the property.

## What is confirmed

- Site: Old City of York Landfill, Pennsylvania.
- EPA ID: `PAD980692420`.
- The property covers approximately 178 acres; about 56 acres were landfilled.
- EPA selected the soil-cover and groundwater remedy in September 1991.
- The remedy included restoration of soil cover in the northeastern landfill area to a minimum thickness of two feet.
- EPA reports that approximately 16 acres of eroded soil cover were restored.
- Other remedy elements include:
  - landfill-gas venting;
  - leachate-vault sediment removal and monitoring;
  - groundwater, surface-water, sediment and seep monitoring;
  - institutional controls.
- Construction was completed in 1996 and EPA issued a Preliminary Close-Out Report.
- Long-term groundwater, surface-water, sediment and landfill-air-vent monitoring continues.
- A private home occupies part of the larger site property.
- EPA's 2026 Five-Year Review found the remedy protective in the short term.
- EPA's stated long-term recommendation from that review is additional PFAS sampling of groundwater, surface water, sediment and leachate media, rather than a reported soil-cover failure.

## Why it is not usable

1. The two-foot value is a minimum remedy requirement, not a mapped point-specific accepted depth.
2. No public construction table showing actual local final cover thicknesses was recovered.
3. No survey coordinates tied to actual depth values were recovered.
4. No numerical measurement precision, construction tolerance, survey accuracy or finite uncertainty interval was recovered.
5. Landfill-gas vents, leachate structures, monitoring locations, access areas and recurring sampling create spatial confounding.
6. Residential occupation on part of the larger property prevents treating the whole site as a uniform unchanged reference.
7. A protective Five-Year Review finding does not convert a design minimum into calibration truth.
