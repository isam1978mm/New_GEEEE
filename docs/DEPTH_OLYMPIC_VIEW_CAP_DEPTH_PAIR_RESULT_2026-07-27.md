# Olympic View Sanitary Landfill cap-depth pair result - 2026-07-27

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO**

Olympic View passes the large-area and long-term-stability screens, but it does not provide the two broad, final measured as-built depth conditions required by the approved numerical-depth plan.

## What was recovered

Official Washington Department of Ecology records were recovered through temporary draft PR #20 and workflow run `30318804097`.

Key records reviewed:

- 2011 Final Cover Investigation and Erosion Repair Report;
- Post-Closure Operation and Maintenance Plan;
- 2017 and 2021 periodic reviews;
- annual monitoring reports through 2024;
- Cleanup Action Plan.

The approximate closed refuse area is 65 acres across the Old Barney White Landfill, Phase I and Phase II. The site has a maintained vegetated cover and a plausible stable Sentinel-1 period.

## Fatal blocker

The 2011 investigation did **not** measure a broad cap-thickness grid.

It excavated ten narrow backhoe test pits along the perimeter landfill-gas header. A typical pit was approximately 50-60 feet long and 4 feet wide. The purpose was to expose and inspect the geosynthetic final-cover components and confirm that the landfill-gas header installation had not damaged them.

The report concluded that the geosynthetic cover remained intact. It did not provide:

- measured final-cover soil thickness values;
- two broad measured depth polygons;
- a pointwise thickness table that could support conservative polygons;
- numerical depth-measurement uncertainty.

## Cover profiles

The Post-Closure Operation and Maintenance Plan describes Phases I and II under one repeated final-cover profile:

- 12 inches vegetative topsoil and cover soil;
- geotextile fabric;
- 12-inch drainage layer;
- geonet composite;
- geomembrane;
- 6-inch low-permeability soil.

This is one design condition, not two different measured depths.

The Old Barney White Landfill has a different closure and upgrade history. Its older soil cover was later upgraded with a geomembrane, geocomposite drainage layer and topsoil. The public records do not provide a coordinate-tied final measured thickness grid proving that it forms a matched near-surface comparison with Phases I-II.

## Stability

The periodic reviews and annual reports support continued inspection, vegetation maintenance and cover integrity. This is useful for the stability gate, but it cannot replace the missing measured depth pair.
