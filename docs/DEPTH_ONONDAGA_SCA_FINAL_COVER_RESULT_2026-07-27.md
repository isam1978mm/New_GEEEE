# Onondaga Lake SCA final-cover result - 2026-07-27

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO**

The Sediment Consolidation Area is a large, completed and vegetated engineered closure. It fails the approved numerical-depth plan because its upland final cover has one documented profile rather than two large measured depth conditions.

## Documented vegetated profile

The official five-year-review record describes construction of the SCA final cover from 2015 through 2017. From top to bottom, the profile is:

- 2 inches average compost;
- 6 inches vegetative soil consisting of 60 percent on-site borrow soil, 30 percent imported sand and 10 percent on-site topsoil;
- 18 inches protective soil;
- geocomposite drainage and underlying barrier components.

This is strong construction documentation, but it represents one vegetated depth condition.

## Fatal blocker: no second vegetated depth zone

The public record does not identify a second broad SCA zone with:

- a different certified final soil depth;
- matching compost and vegetative-soil construction;
- coordinate-tied measured thickness values;
- enough clean interior for a separate Sentinel-1 condition.

Without a second depth or exact negative zone, the SCA cannot create a shallow/deep calibration pair.

## Why the other Onondaga thickness measurements cannot be used

The wider Onondaga Lake remedy contains many alternative cap profiles. It also includes extensive bathymetric surveys, probing and 177 verification cores.

Those measurements apply to submerged lake-bottom cap areas. They are not measurements of the upland vegetated SCA final cover.

The submerged areas have a fundamentally different radar-facing condition and fall outside the user-approved full-scale vegetated-cover scope. They cannot be substituted for a second SCA depth zone.
