## What the public record proves

The Faro Mine Landform, Cover and Revegetation Pilot is a real field-scale construction program, not a conceptual design.

The available sources establish that:

- Phase 1 of the landform and revegetation pilot was completed in 2022;
- 15 cover trial panels were constructed in the Mine Area;
- the Mine Area panels test different cover materials, nominal cover thicknesses, and placement methods;
- the Mine Area includes plateau panels at about 5% slope and side-slope panels at about 33% slope;
- six geotechnical trial pads of varying nominal thickness were constructed in the Tailings Area;
- construction included excavation, hauling, placing, and compacting borrow materials;
- GPS-equipped machinery was used to build to design and to track compaction passes;
- geotextiles and geotechnical monitoring equipment were installed;
- quality-control check sheets, progress tracking, and site inspections were part of construction;
- the panels are intended for continued monitoring to inform the final cover design.

These facts make Faro a technically serious and potentially valuable evidence source.

## Why Faro still fails the Option 1 calibration gate

The public sources do not provide the exact information needed to create a defensible numerical-depth calibration record.

### 1. No public panel-by-panel as-built depth table

The public record states that different thicknesses were built, but it does not expose a table identifying:

- each panel or pad;
- its final measured constructed thickness;
- the measurement locations or coverage;
- whether the value applies across the full polygon;
- the difference between design thickness and achieved as-built thickness.

The existence of GPS-guided construction does not, by itself, prove a final measured polygon-level depth value.

### 2. No numerical thickness uncertainty

The public sources do not state:

- vertical survey accuracy;
- construction-thickness tolerance;
- confidence interval;
- standard deviation;
- measurement error;
- acceptance range for the final panel thickness.

GPS use and compaction-pass tracking are quality-control evidence, but they are not a published numerical uncertainty for final cover depth.

### 3. No official coordinate-tied treatment polygons

The public record does not provide usable:

- GIS polygons;
- CAD files;
- surveyed panel corners;
- northing/easting tables;
- coordinate system and datum;
- coordinate-controlled as-built drawings for every panel.

A general mine map, photograph, or satellite interpretation cannot replace official treatment geometry.

### 4. No clean matched shallow/deep pair is publicly established

The 15 Mine Area panels intentionally vary more than depth. The public description includes changes in:

- cover material;
- cover thickness;
- placement method;
- slope class;
- revegetation and implementation method.

Therefore a shallow/deep pair cannot be selected without the unpublished treatment matrix and construction details. Any radar difference could reflect material, placement, slope, roughness, moisture, vegetation, or drainage rather than depth.

### 5. Stable Sentinel-1 period is not yet proven for exact panels

The pilot was constructed in late 2022 and is under continued monitoring. Broader Faro remediation, revegetation, infrastructure work, and adaptive management are still active.

The public record does not provide plot-specific proof that an exact candidate pair remained unchanged through a sufficient Sentinel-1 observation period after excluding:

- planting and revegetation work;
- instrumentation activity;
- repairs or regrading;
- access traffic;
- erosion or drainage changes;
- nearby construction effects.

## Scientific consequence

Faro may contain the needed information in non-public construction records, including GPS machine-control files, quality-control check sheets, as-built survey deliverables, and the full treatment matrix.

However, those records are not available in the reviewed public package. The app cannot infer them from imagery or from the statement that construction followed design.

## Exact reopening condition

Do not reopen Faro unless a concrete official package becomes available containing all or most of:

1. the complete panel and pad treatment matrix;
2. final measured as-built thickness for each candidate panel;
3. numerical construction or survey uncertainty;
4. official GIS, CAD, or surveyed corner coordinates;
5. surface material, placement, vegetation, slope, drainage, and maintenance details for a matched pair;
6. plot-specific monitoring and repair history after construction.
