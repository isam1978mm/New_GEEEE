# Option 1 — Global Depth batch 3 cheap screen — 2026-07-28

## Decision

One candidate advanced to decisive review: **Fort Dix Landfill, New Jersey**.

The other four candidates stopped at the cheap documentary screen. No Earth Engine query was run.

## Locked screen requirements

A candidate advances only when public records plausibly support:

- completed construction;
- a full-scale vegetated area with at least 30–40 m clean interior potential;
- two distinct numerical depth conditions, or one numerical condition plus a confirmed no-target control;
- exact geometry likely recoverable;
- comparable radar-facing surfaces;
- a stable Sentinel-1 period;
- final measured as-built depth and numerical uncertainty likely available.

## Candidate results

### 1. Fort Dix Landfill, New Jersey

**ADVANCE TO DECISIVE REVIEW**

Official EPA records establish:

- a 126-acre landfill;
- a remedy requiring an engineered cap over the southernmost 53 acres;
- maintenance of two feet of existing final cover over the remaining landfill;
- Phase II completion in fiscal year 1997;
- long-term cap inspection, mowing, erosion control and site security;
- a 2023 five-year review finding no condition changes affecting protectiveness.

Why it advanced:

- both areas are large;
- the site has a long stable management period;
- the remedy language appears at first to identify two cover conditions.

Decisive questions:

1. Is the two-foot northern cover a final measured as-built depth or only a maintenance requirement?
2. Is the southern engineered cap tied to a final measured depth polygon and uncertainty?
3. Are the radar-facing vegetation and upper soil assemblies comparable?
4. Can either area serve as a confirmed control?

Official sources:

- EPA Fort Dix cleanup profile: https://cumulis.epa.gov/supercpad/SiteProfiles/index.cfm?fuseaction=second.cleanup&id=0201164
- EPA Record of Decision: https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=9100Q06P.TXT

### 2. Hunt's Disposal Landfill, Wisconsin

**STOP — one cap system only**

EPA's 2025 public notice describes one multi-layered cap, a slurry wall, groundwater treatment, gas management and institutional controls. The public description does not identify a second measured depth condition or confirmed control area.

Official source:

- https://www.epa.gov/publicnotices/hunts-disposal-landfill-five-year-review

### 3. Strasburg Landfill, Pennsylvania

**STOP — one cap system only**

EPA states that the long-term remedy included construction of a multi-layer cap, completed in 1999, with continuing operation and maintenance. The public record reviewed for this screen does not identify two different final measured depth polygons or a confirmed control.

Official source:

- https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-completes-clean-and-removes-strasburg-landfill-site-national-superfund-list

### 4. Doepke-Holliday Superfund Site, Kansas

**STOP — one impermeable multilayer cap**

EPA records describe one impermeable multilayer cap over the waste-disposal area. The site is stable and maintained, but no second measured depth condition or confirmed no-target control is documented in the reviewed public material.

Official sources:

- https://archive.epa.gov/region07/factsheets/web/html/3rd_five_yr_review_doepke-holliday_johnson_co_ks.html
- https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1019UVG.txt

### 5. Bell Landfill, Pennsylvania

**STOP — two landfill caps, but no public numerical depth pair**

EPA confirms that two on-site landfills were capped and that the remedy was completed in 2000. The reviewed public profile does not provide two different measured final depths, numerical uncertainty, or a confirmed control area. Two capped landfills are not automatically a usable depth pair.

Official source:

- https://cumulis.epa.gov/supercpad/SiteProfiles/index.cfm?fuseaction=second.cleanup&id=0301509
