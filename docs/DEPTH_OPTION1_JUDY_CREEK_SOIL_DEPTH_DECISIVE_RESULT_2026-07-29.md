# Option 1 decisive result — Judy Creek Test Mine soil-depth experiment

Date: 2026-07-29

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO for numerical-depth calibration.**

This is **Option 1 evidence research**. Option 3 is not active.

## What the public technical record proves

The Judy Creek Test Mine study is a genuine replicated soil-depth experiment:

- nine plots were established in a randomized-block design;
- three replacement-depth treatments were used: 0 cm, 30 cm and 70 cm;
- the replaced material was Grey Luvisol surface soil containing mixed A and B horizons;
- the common underlying material was till subsoil;
- the plots were seeded with a common grass/legume program and planted with lodgepole pine;
- monitoring ran from 1979 through 1983 and evaluated vegetation cover and tree response.

The candidate therefore passes the cheap scientific-design screen for a same-material depth contrast.

## Fatal blockers

The accessible public record does not provide the execution evidence required by Option 1:

- no plot dimensions are published in the indexed report summary;
- the primary PDF was publicly linked but could not be recovered from the available public cache;
- no conservative 30–40 m clean interior can therefore be established for any one treatment plot;
- no certified as-built thickness grid, same-coordinate survey, or numerical construction uncertainty is available;
- no official surveyed corners, GIS/CAD polygons, datum or coordinate-controlled treatment map is published;
- the study record ends in 1983 and does not prove that the exact plots remained identifiable, unchanged and repair-free during a usable Sentinel-1 period beginning in 2014.

A scientific treatment label is not sufficient to assign an execution polygon an exact physical depth. Judy Creek cannot advance without both size and ground-truth evidence.

## Reopening condition

Reopen only if an official or operator-issued package supplies:

1. plot dimensions and coordinate-controlled boundaries;
2. measured final 30 cm and 70 cm thicknesses with numerical uncertainty;
3. clean interiors after boundary, tree and monitoring exclusions; and
4. mapped post-2014 land-use and stability records for the exact plots.
