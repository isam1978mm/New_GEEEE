# Option 1 decisive result — MacKenzie–Naeth forest-soil propagule-bank experiment

Date: 2026-07-29

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO for numerical-depth calibration.**

This is **Option 1 evidence research**. Option 3 is not active.

## What the public record proves

The experiment evaluated reclamation using two surface-soil materials at two application depths:

- forest-floor material containing litter, fragmented litter, humus, and mineral soil;
- peat material;
- approximately 10 cm and 20 cm placement depths;
- field vegetation and soil response assessed after placement;
- an operationally relevant field component intended to evaluate natural plant establishment.

## Fatal blockers

- The experiment is a material-by-depth factorial design. Forest-floor material and peat have substantially different near-surface properties and propagule communities.
- Later primary reporting states that the apparent response to 10 cm versus 20 cm forest-floor placement was not attributed simply to depth; operational placement caused different amounts of fine-textured subsoil to be mixed into the upper layer.
- The accessible public journal and thesis records do not publish plot dimensions sufficient to establish a clean 30–40 m radar interior.
- No certified final placement-depth survey, numerical construction uncertainty, or official coordinate-tied treatment polygons are published.
- The reported ecological observation period is short and does not prove that exact treatment interiors remained identifiable and unchanged during a Sentinel-1 period beginning in 2014.

The material and equipment-mixing confounds are decisive even before satellite analysis.
