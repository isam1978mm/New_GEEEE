# Option 1 decisive result — Stanton, North Dakota soil-wedge experiment

Date: 2026-07-29

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO for numerical-depth calibration.**

This is **Option 1 evidence research**. Option 3 is not active.

## What the official public record proves

The USDA-ARS Stanton experiment is a genuine long-term reconstructed-soil depth study:

- it was initiated in 1974 near Stanton, North Dakota;
- a single subsoil wedge was constructed over levelled sodic mine spoil;
- the wedge was approximately 240 m long, 65 m wide and 2.1 m high at its summit;
- it contained 12 long contiguous main-treatment blocks: three replications of four treatments;
- the topsoil treatments included 0 cm, 20 cm and 60 cm, plus one mixed subsoil–topsoil treatment;
- each main treatment block was split into four long crop strips;
- the crops were alfalfa, crested wheatgrass, spring wheat and a warm-season grass mix;
- intensive soil measurements were collected from 1976 through 1981;
- later studies revisited the experiment approximately 24 to 28 years after construction;
- the USDA dataset publishes overall Stanton and Zap site coordinates and preserves the historical soil data.

## Fatal blockers

The treatment geometry is too narrow for the approved radar-support gate.

- The published Stanton layout identifies each crop subplot as approximately 4 m wide.
- Four crop strips therefore occupy only about 16 m of cross-treatment width, or at most roughly 16–20 m allowing for small internal separations.
- This is below the required 30–40 m clean interior before excluding crop boundaries, wedge edges, erosion features, sampling points and other disturbance.
- The overall 240 m × 65 m wedge size does not make each individual 20 cm or 60 cm treatment radar-sized.

The remaining evidence gates also fail:

- the 20 cm and 60 cm values are construction-treatment labels, not a published certified plot-wide as-built thickness surface;
- no numerical construction-thickness or survey uncertainty is attached to the treatment labels;
- the public spatial metadata identifies the overall experimental sites, not official coordinate-controlled treatment polygons or surveyed treatment corners;
- the wedge also varies continuously in subsoil thickness and topographic position, and each topsoil treatment is split among different crops;
- the long-term revisits occurred about 24–28 years after the 1974 establishment, rather than proving that exact treatment strips remained unchanged and repair-free after Sentinel-1 operations began in 2014.

The size failure alone is decisive. No Earth Engine comparison is justified.
