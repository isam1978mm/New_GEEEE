# Option 1 decisive result — Syncrude forest-topsoil salvage and placement-depth trial

Date: 2026-07-29

## Decision

**NOT GOOD TO GO for numerical-depth calibration.**

This is **Option 1 evidence research**. Option 3 is not active.

## What the primary public paper proves

The operational-scale trial tested forest-topsoil handling at a Syncrude oil-sands reclamation site north of Fort McMurray:

- forest topsoil was salvaged at 10 cm and 25 cm depths;
- the material was placed at 10 cm and 20 cm depths;
- treatments were constructed on two different substrates: approximately 1 m of sand and approximately 1 m of a 50:50 peat-mineral mix;
- each treatment combination was replicated three times;
- each replicate measured approximately 15 m by 70 m;
- vegetation and soil response were assessed in years 1 and 3;
- operational equipment was used for construction.

## Fatal blockers

- The 15 m plot width is far below the required 30–40 m clean radar interior before any boundary exclusion.
- Salvage depth, placement depth, and substrate type all vary within the factorial design, so many comparisons are not depth-only comparisons.
- The paper notes that operational equipment and large-scale construction reduce placement precision.
- No certified as-built placement-depth survey, numerical construction uncertainty, or official coordinate-tied treatment polygons are published.
- Three years of ecological monitoring does not establish a long, repair-free Sentinel-1 observation interval for the exact plots.

The size failure is decisive. No Earth Engine work is justified.
