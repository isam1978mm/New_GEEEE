# River Road Landfill — Surveyed Certification-Pit Candidate

Date: 2026-07-25

## Decision

**Status:** highest-priority measured-depth candidate found so far, not yet a usable calibration row.

River Road is unusually strong because its 1987 closure certification documents dense, direct physical verification of the final soil cover. A total of 129 certification pits were excavated across the landfill, their locations were surveyed, and the engineering field reports recorded the final cover thickness at each pit. Deficient areas were corrected and re-certified. The site later remained inactive, undeveloped, protected by institutional controls, and subject to recurring inspections.

The candidate still cannot enter the calibration pack because the individual pit-depth records and the surveyed pit-location sheet have not been extracted, and no numerical measurement or survey uncertainty has been found.

## What is confirmed

- Site: River Road Landfill, Hermitage and South Pymatuning Township, Mercer County, Pennsylvania; EPA ID PAD000439083.
- The site contains approximately 37.5 acres of refuse disposal within a 102-acre property.
- Waste disposal stopped in 1986.
- The PADEP-approved closure was completed and professionally certified in 1987.
- The closure report states that a minimum of three feet (0.9144 m) of final soil cover was emplaced.
- Approximately two backhoe pits per acre were used during construction to verify the required three-foot cover thickness.
- A total of 129 final cover-certification pits were excavated.
- The certification-pit locations were surveyed and shown on the final-cover certification drawing, Sheet 1 of 3.
- Todd Giddings and Associates field reports in Appendix A record the final cover thickness for each pit.
- Areas found deficient were corrected and re-certified using overlapping pits and/or documented visual inspection.
- A less-than-one-acre knob in the extreme southeast was not investigated and must be excluded.
- Soil from the pits was tested for textural classification and nutrient requirements.
- The cover work received construction management, engineering inspection, QA/QC certification, and surveying support.
- The facility design and construction were certified by a registered professional engineer.
- Survey control verified required elevations for the erosion and sediment-control works.
- Horizontal and vertical site control were tied to permanent physical markers.
- Annual topographic surveys were required to use the same grid system and to bear the seal of a registered professional engineer or surveyor.
- By 1995, EPA investigations described the cap as structurally sound, free of cracks, deformities, major depressions, and seeps, with depth and soil type generally consistent with the closure plan.
- The selected federal remedy preserved and maintained the existing cap rather than constructing a replacement cap.
- Deed and land-use controls restrict soil excavation, protect the cap, and prohibit residential construction.
- The landfill remains inactive and has no identified economic redevelopment use.
- The site was deleted from the NPL in 2004.
- The August 9, 2024 five-year review concluded that the remedy remained protective in the short term because the landfill remained capped, fenced, and controlled.
- EPA's stated long-term recommendation in the public 2024 summary concerns surface-water and sediment sampling, not a reported cap failure.

## Why it is not yet usable

1. The 129 individual pit-depth values have not been extracted from Appendix A.
2. The surveyed final-cover certification drawing has not been recovered in readable form.
3. Point identifiers have not been matched to exact survey coordinates and the coordinate reference system.
4. No numerical pit-depth measurement uncertainty, survey tolerance, or finite two-sided depth interval has been recovered.
5. The certified three-foot value is a minimum and must not replace the actual recorded pit values.
6. Pits that initially failed and were corrected require the final re-certification record, not the original deficient measurement.
7. The uninvestigated southeast knob must be excluded.
8. Diversion berms, lined channels, riprap, sedimentation structures, leachate infrastructure, gas probes, monitoring wells, access routes, and later maintenance areas must be excluded.
9. Later inspection and maintenance records must be overlaid with each candidate pit to prove an unchanged Sentinel-1 interval.
10. The old pit measurements must be interpreted as depth to the top of refuse or the pre-cover surface; the field forms must confirm the measurement endpoint.

## Required next steps

1. Recover Appendix A field reports from the closure certification.
2. Extract the final accepted value for every certification pit.
3. Recover Sheet 1 of 3 and its survey notes.
4. Record privately for each candidate pit:
   - pit identifier;
   - exact survey location;
   - coordinate reference system and datum;
   - measured final cover thickness;
   - measurement endpoint;
   - inspection date;
   - whether the value was an original pass or a re-certification;
   - documented measurement and survey uncertainty.
5. Exclude the southeast knob and all engineered or disturbed corridors.
6. Recover the 2004, 2009, 2014, 2019, and 2024 inspection/maintenance histories.
7. Match only unchanged pit neighborhoods to a stable Sentinel-1 observation interval.
8. Add private calibration records only after the validator's depth, geometry, uncertainty, and evidence fields pass.
