# Strasburg Landfill — Design-Only Cover and Active Remedy Infrastructure

Date: 2026-07-26

## Decision

**Status:** closed as unsuitable for numerical depth calibration.

Strasburg Landfill has a completed multi-layer engineered cap and remains undeveloped, but the public record provides design-layer requirements rather than mapped, point-specific accepted cover depths. The cap also contains landfill-gas and leachate infrastructure and remains under continuing operation and maintenance. No numerical measurement or survey uncertainty was recovered.

## What is confirmed

- Site: Strasburg Landfill, Chester County, Pennsylvania.
- EPA ID: `PAD000441337`.
- The site includes an approximately 24-acre inactive landfill on a larger undeveloped property.
- The OU3 remedy included:
  - regrading the landfill;
  - construction of a multi-layer cap;
  - a subsurface leachate collection system;
  - leachate treatment;
  - landfill-gas collection/venting;
  - monitoring and institutional controls.
- Construction occurred from August 1996 through September 1999.
- EPA approved completion of the OU3 remedial action on September 29, 2000.
- Approximately 600,000 cubic yards of earthen material were placed during reconstruction.
- The 1992 Record of Decision describes the selected cap profile using approximate design layers, including compacted clay, synthetic membrane, sand and topsoil/loam.
- The gas flare operated from 1999 to 2016.
- EPA approved conversion to passive landfill-gas venting in April 2016.
- PADEP remains responsible for operation, maintenance and monitoring.
- Activity and use restrictions prohibit disturbance of the landfill remedy.
- A conservation easement protects substantial surrounding woodland and habitat.
- The 2025 remedy amendment primarily addresses contaminated residential drinking-water wells and does not establish new cap-depth measurements.

## Why it is not usable

1. The published layer thicknesses are remedy-design values, not point-specific accepted as-built measurements.
2. No public table of local final cover thickness, inspection points or construction test results was recovered.
3. No mapped point locations tied to actual depth values were recovered.
4. No numerical construction tolerance, survey accuracy, measurement precision or finite uncertainty interval was recovered.
5. Gas vents, leachate collection infrastructure, monitoring points, drainage features and access/maintenance areas create surface confounding.
6. Conversion from an active flare system to passive venting in 2016 changed remedy infrastructure during the Sentinel-1 era.
7. A protective and undeveloped site is not automatically an unchanged radar reference.
8. The 2025–2026 drinking-water action does not repair the missing depth, geometry or uncertainty evidence.
