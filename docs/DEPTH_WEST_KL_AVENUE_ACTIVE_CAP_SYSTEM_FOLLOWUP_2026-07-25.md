# West KL Avenue Landfill — Active Cap-System Follow-up

Date: 2026-07-25

## Decision

**Status:** closed as a numerical-depth calibration route.

West KL Avenue has a completed multilayer impermeable landfill cap, but the cap is part of an actively operated gas-extraction and monitoring system rather than a simple unchanged surface. Public records confirm trenching, active gas collection, an enclosed flare, regular inspection and maintenance, and extensive ongoing groundwater work. No public point-specific as-built cover thickness, surveyed layer-interface table, or numerical construction uncertainty was recovered.

## What is confirmed

- Site: K&L Avenue Landfill, Oshtemo Township, Kalamazoo County, Michigan; EPA ID MID980506463.
- The property covers approximately 87 acres and the landfill closed in 1979.
- The original 1980 cap had sparse or absent vegetation in places and included subsidence depressions where water ponded.
- EPA later selected an impermeable landfill-cap remedy.
- A state-of-the-art multilayer impermeable cap was constructed during 2005 and 2006.
- The cap includes geosynthetic clay liner and textured liner components.
- Construction completion was recorded on December 20, 2006.
- An enhanced landfill-gas extraction system was installed in 2007.
- Passive vents were converted to active extraction in March 2008.
- The active system uses gas-extraction piping and trenches and thermally destroys extracted gas in an enclosed flare.
- The cap, fence and gas system are inspected and maintained regularly.
- Operation and maintenance remain ongoing.
- The March 20, 2025 Fourth Five-Year Review concluded that the cleanup continued to be protective.
- Groundwater and PFAS investigations and monitoring remained active through 2025.
- The EPA site-document page lists a 13-document key collection and a 595-document administrative record, but the collection tables repeatedly timed out or returned cache misses in the available environment.

## Why it is not usable

1. No point-specific measured final cover thickness was recovered.
2. No surveyed layer-interface elevations, exact usable subarea polygons or numerical construction uncertainty were recovered.
3. A general multilayer-cap design cannot be substituted for an actual measured depth label.
4. Gas-extraction trenches and piping directly disturb the cap and create strong radar and surface confounders.
5. Active extraction wells, flare infrastructure, monitoring wells, access routes and maintenance zones would require detailed exclusion geometry.
6. The cap is inspected and maintained as an operating system, so the full surface cannot be treated as unchanged without point-level maintenance records.
7. Pre-remedy subsidence and ponding show that historical cover conditions were spatially variable.
8. The 2025 protectiveness conclusion confirms remedy performance, not numerical depth or unchanged point-level geometry.
